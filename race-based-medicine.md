“Carrier coverage” is estimated as (1-(1-f)^2). It assumes Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium and shouldn’t be treated as a formal continental coverage calculation.

Some higher-coverage alleles are also useful, but the geographic separation is less clean:

* [C*17:01](https://www.allelefrequencies.net/hla6002a.asp?all_name=C%2A17%3A01): SSA median 8.7%, China maximum 0.53%, but Europe reaches 3.8%.
* [B*15:03](https://www.allelefrequencies.net/hla6002a.asp?all_name=B%2A15%3A03): SSA median 7.7%, China maximum 0.37%, Europe reaches 2.4%.
* [B*45:01](https://www.allelefrequencies.net/hla6002a.asp?all_name=B%2A45%3A01): SSA median 5.2%, China maximum 0.5%, Europe reaches 1.6%.
* [B*53:01](https://www.allelefrequencies.net/hla6002a.asp?all_name=B%2A53%3A01): SSA median 8.75%, China up to 1%, Europe up to 2.36%.

My practical panels would be:

* **Africa versus Europe:** B*42:01, A*74:01, B*58:02, C*02:10, B*81:01
* **Africa versus China:** A*30:02, B*42:01, A*74:01, B*58:02, C*02:10

Don’t add the individual coverage percentages together. Many people carry several of these alleles, and HLA linkage means combined coverage needs haplotype-level calculation. These findings also apply to **Sub-Saharan Africa**, not North Africa. AFND treats those as separate regions in its [geographic classification](https://www.allelefrequencies.net/datasets.asp).
