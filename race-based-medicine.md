### What the numbers mean

**Median SSA allele frequency**

This is the median frequency among positive Sub-Saharan African AFND datasets that met these filters:

* Gold or Silver quality
* At least 100 people
* Two-field HLA typing, such as B*42:01

It is not a population-weighted average for the entire continent. “7.5%” means about 7.5% of the HLA-B gene copies in those datasets were B*42:01.

**Approximate carriers**

People have two copies of each HLA locus. Therefore, allele frequency and the percentage of people carrying the allele are different.

I estimated carriers using:

[
\text{carrier percentage}=1-(1-f)^2
]

For example, B*42:01 at a 7.5% allele frequency gives:

[
1-(1-0.075)^2=14.4%
]

So approximately 14 people in 100 would carry at least one B*42:01 allele in a population with that frequency.

**Highest Europe and China frequency**

These are the highest AFND allele frequencies found in any qualifying two-field dataset with at least 100 people. They are deliberately conservative, worst-case overlap values, not typical European or Chinese frequencies.

For example:

* B*42:01: 7.5% median SSA frequency, but no more than 0.1% in either comparison.
* A*30:02: 8.3% in SSA and only 0.4% in China, but as high as 4.29% in Europe. That makes it useful against China, not Europe.

**“None reported”**

This means AFND contained no positive Chinese report meeting the filters. It does not prove that the allele is biologically absent from every Chinese population.

These results cover **Sub-Saharan Africa**. North Africa is a separate AFND region and would require a different analysis.
